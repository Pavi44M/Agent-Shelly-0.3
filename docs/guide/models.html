Forecasts: how good are they?

Forecast accuracy is measured, not assumed. The same approach as Pavi's MAB thesis, where seasonal naive beat XGBoost.

At a glance

  • Rolling-origin backtest: 4 folds × 7 days
  • Scored by WAPE (total absolute error ÷ total actual) and MAPE
  • Best model chosen per category
  • Cross-checked independently in R

How it works

  1. SARIMA-X: order (1,0,1)(1,1,1)[7] with a NZ public-holiday regressor, fitted on the last 364 days. If it becomes numerically unstable, it falls back to seasonal naive.
  2. XGBoost: one global model across categories with weekday, day-of-year, holiday and lagged features (lags ≥ 7, so a 7-day forecast needs no recursion).
  3. Baselines: last value, same day last week, and drift.

Method & formulas

WAPE
Σ|actual − forecast| ÷ Σ actual × 100
MAPE
mean(|actual − forecast| ÷ actual) × 100 (skips zero days)

How to read it

  • If the best model only beats seasonal naive by a point or two, the extra complexity isn't worth much.
  • A sudden jump in a model's error is flagged by the learning loop as possible drift.

Limits & safeguards

  • No weather or promo calendar yet. Both are on the roadmap as model inputs.
