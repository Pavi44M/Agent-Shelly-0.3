Product segments (K-means)

Segmentation turns 50 SKUs into a handful of groups that each need a different strategy.

At a glance

  • 6 standardised features over 13 weeks
  • k chosen by silhouette score (2–5)
  • Bootstrap Jaccard stability over 100 resamples: under 0.6 unstable, 0.6–0.75 a pattern, 0.75–0.85 stable, over 0.85 highly stable
  • Segments named by what makes them most distinctive

How it works

  1. Features are standardised (z-scores) so no single unit dominates.
  2. K-means runs for k = 2…5 and the best silhouette is kept.
  3. PCA projects the 6 dimensions onto 2 for the chart only.
  4. The R validation script re-checks with the gap statistic and MANOVA.

Method & formulas

Silhouette
(b − a) ÷ max(a, b), where a = mean distance within a cluster and b = to the nearest other cluster
Jaccard
|A ∩ B| ÷ |A ∪ B| between original and resampled clusters

How to read it

  • Treat unstable segments as hypotheses. Shelly says so rather than overselling.
  • Waste-exposed perishables → tighter ordering and earlier markdowns. Traffic drivers → never out of stock.

Limits & safeguards

  • On demo data the silhouette is modest (~0.28) and the gap statistic prefers 2 clusters. That's reported honestly.
