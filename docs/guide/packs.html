Industry packs: beyond retail

Each pack is a set of skills with its own demo data, findings and decision proposals. Swap in real data through connectors.

At a glance

  • Electronics: sell-through, weeks of cover, aged-stock ladder with a below-cost markdown check, ASP erosion, accessory attach rate
  • Wholesale: customer contribution after cost-to-serve, AR ageing and DSO, fill rate and OTIF
  • Warehousing: ABC (velocity) × XYZ (variability) slotting, lines per hour with coaching flags, weeks until 95% full
  • Production: OEE = availability × performance × quality, scrap cost, schedule adherence

How it works

  1. Run a pack: python -m shelly pack electronics (or wholesale, warehousing, production).
  2. Findings become actions; major ones become decisions for confirmation.
  3. Full reports are in docs/example/pack_*.md.

Method & formulas

Weeks of cover
on hand ÷ average weekly units (4 weeks)
Cost-to-serve
orders × cost per order ($18 default)
DSO
receivables ÷ 90-day sales incl. GST × 90
OEE
(run time ÷ planned) × (units ÷ (run time × ideal rate)) × (good ÷ units)

How to read it

  • In electronics, stock loses value every week it sits, so aged stock and weeks of cover matter more than turnover.
  • In wholesale, the biggest customers aren't always the most profitable once cost-to-serve is included.
  • In production, fix the biggest OEE loss first (availability, speed or quality).

Limits & safeguards

  • Pack demo data is synthetic. Cost assumptions (cost per order, unit cost) are placeholders to replace.
